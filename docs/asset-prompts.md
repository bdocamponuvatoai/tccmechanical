# Tabor's Complete Comfort — image direction

These prompts define the visual direction for generated site photography. The images are illustrative scenes, not photographs of Tabor's employees or completed projects.

## Shared direction

- Natural documentary photography of residential HVAC work in Chicago homes.
- Anonymous technicians in plain charcoal workwear, shown from the side, from behind, or cropped above the mouth.
- Honest materials, technically plausible equipment, restrained color, soft natural light, and subtle grain.
- Chicago cues should come from ordinary architecture: brick two-flats, bungalows, painted masonry basements, oak trim, and black iron fences.
- No readable text, logos, brand names, uniform patches, watermarks, staged smiles, handshakes, glowing airflow, dramatic damage, glossy CGI, hard HDR, teal-orange grading, lens flare, or implausible tools.

## Cooling — `cooling.webp`

Documentary editorial photograph of an anonymous HVAC technician servicing an open central-air condenser beside a classic Chicago brick two-flat on a warm summer morning. The technician kneels in plain charcoal workwear and checks manifold gauges. Use a narrow city side yard with a black iron fence and established greenery. Compose as a 3:2 landscape that also crops well to 4:3 and square.

## Indoor air quality — `air-quality.webp`

Candid editorial photograph inside a modest, well-kept Chicago bungalow. An anonymous technician carefully replaces a pleated HVAC filter at a hallway return cabinet while holding the used filter nearby. Include warm off-white plaster, oak trim, a small air-quality meter with its screen turned away, and soft window daylight. Compose as a 3:2 landscape that crops well to 4:3 and square.

## Water heaters — `water-heaters.webp`

Documentary editorial photograph in the clean but genuinely used basement utility area of an older Chicago home. An anonymous technician uses an inspection light and small tool at the lower access panel of a modern unbranded tank-style water heater. Include painted masonry, exposed joists, copper piping, and a floor drain. Use soft basement-window daylight mixed with one warm practical light. Compose as a 3:2 landscape that crops well to 4:3 and square.
